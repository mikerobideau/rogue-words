# Disc Engine

You're a DJ playing shows against the crowd. Each gig is a short fight over several turns: you play a few CDs per turn, their points are damage, and the crowd fights back with a move it shows you ahead of time. Get the crowd's HP to 0 and you win them over.

**Prototype:** run `nine_pocket.tscn` (F6). Constants are in `nine_pocket_data.gd`. Rules are in `nine_pocket.gd`: turns in `_start_turn` / `_end_turn` / `_roll_move`, show flow in `_start_show` / `_on_pocket` / `_on_slot` / `_play` / `_start_slot` / `_finish_song` / `_end_show`, scoring in `_score_ctx` / `_score_slot` / `_score_set`. Shared drawing helpers are in `nine_pocket_ui.gd`. Earlier versions are in git history.

Loop: gig → record shop → gig → record shop → ... → gig, `RUN_GIGS` (6) gigs in all with a random venue each. The fork in the road is switched off (`USE_FORK` false) until it is redesigned for the new combat. Getting booed off (HP left after the last turn) ends the run.

## The gig (turns)
- **Crowd HP is the gig's target** (`GIG_TARGETS`, rising each gig). The points your songs score are damage. The paper on the stereo shows the HP left, the turn, and the crowd's next move. Winning is checked at the end of each turn, and finishing early pays `TURN_BONUS` ($1) per turn left.
- **A turn:** the tray has **4 slots** (`SETLIST`). You may fill any number of them (at least one), leaving gaps on purpose, and press PLAY. The CDs resolve left to right, each playing its own song (placing or hovering a CD is silent). Then the played CDs go to the discard pile, your hand refills to 8, and the next turn starts. A gig lasts `TURNS` (4) turns. There is 1 discard per gig. PLAY starts the first song at once, with no countdown.
- **Scoring a turn (beats, then one multiply).** Each song plays a series of scoring beats: its base points, then its effects, editions and gear. Each beat adds to the song's points (shown on its CD) and the turn's **points** readout, and any flow effect raises the turn's **flow**. Every update pulses the readout and the CD. When the last song is done, the finale runs: the turn's points are **multiplied by flow** once, streamed into the **score**, and that is the damage to the crowd's HP. The CDs keep their own tallies (points and flow they added) so you can see where the total came from.
- **Flow resets to x1 every turn** (plus any stored flow from gear) and never cools during the turn. A card can instead bank an effect for **next turn** (target "Start of next turn" in the card workshop): points added to next turn's points, flow added to or multiplied into next turn's starting flow, or cash paid when the next turn starts. Banked effects from the last turn of a gig are lost.
- **Crowd moves.** At the start of every turn the crowd shows one negative move (`MOVE_WEIGHTS`). They replace the old likes, hates and requests.
  - *Cools off:* deal at least 20% of the gig's HP this turn or the crowd wins back 12%.
  - *Boo:* songs of one genre or decade (picked from your hand) score half this turn.
  - *Demand:* play a song of that genre or decade this turn or the crowd wins back 12%.
  - *Static:* your flow above x1 is cut in half before the turn plays.
  - *Heckler:* one slot is blocked and cannot be used this turn.
  - Gear: **Noise Gate** ignores the first boo each gig, **Request Line** pays points when a demand is met.
- **Conditions** that read neighbors work on the whole tray, since everything is placed before PLAY. The old "set 1 / set 2" and "above" conditions are gone, replaced by **next slot is empty**, **previous slot is empty** and **per empty slot** (a blocked slot does not count as empty). Cards that used them were converted (Rock Anthem, Power Ballad; the "above" cards now read "the song after is").
- **Removed:** the Flow-at-end scoring mode, the One-at-a-time placement mode and their Settings. The finale animation code (`_tick_finale`) is still in the file but unused.

## Fork in the road (switched off)
The map is switched off by `USE_FORK` in the data file (set it false to bring the map and `_make_tour` back; their code is intact). A run starts at the first fork after you pick a kit. A tour is one fork and `LANE_GIGS` (3) gigs; after the last headliner of the last tour the run is won.

- **A fork** is two lane signs side by side on a dusk highway, shown at the start of each tour. Click a sign to take that lane.
- **A lane** is three gigs in order, with a record shop after every gig, including the headliner. Lanes are not themed: the gigs are random venues (no venue repeats across the two lanes) and the third gig is the headliner. The sign lists each gig with one liked and one hated tag (hover for the full likes and hates) and the headliner's challenge.
- **The headliner** is a boss gig (see Boss gigs and fatigue) with its own challenge from `BOSS_KINDS`; the two lanes get different challenges.
- **Pickups and tip jars are off** (`PICKUPS_ENABLED` is false). Their code is intact; setting it true adds rolled pickup / tip jar stops before each gig in a lane, though the fork sign does not list them.
- Target growth and pay work as before. Gig number rises with each gig played, so the final headliner is gig 6.

## Tour map
Modeled on Wildfrost: gig → a couple of rewards → gig. You start on the map (titled "Tour Map", with a legend using the real pin symbols), a worn road atlas spread on the desk (fold creases, lake and river, state lines, coffee ring, tape). Generated fresh each run in `_make_tour`:
- **Columns:** **Hometown** (the start) → Gig → Reward → Reward → Gig → Reward → Reward → Gig → Reward → Reward → **Headliner**. Every route plays **3 gigs plus the headliner**.
- **Gig columns** have 1–2 venues (red pins) so you can choose a crowd. On the map a gig shows its name, town and a **hint**: one liked tag (green, "+ Rock") and one hated tag (red, "- Jazz"); the rest stay hidden until you hover.
- **Reward columns** have 2–3 stops, and each pair is built so the build can never stall on luck. One column of the pair holds the **record shop** plus possibly one **cash** stop (50%), with any other stop a **pickup**. The other column is **all pickups**. So every route between two gigs passes at least one pickup (a free CD or gear), and cash can never appear twice in a row. Checked over 3,000 generated tours: 0 routes without a pickup and 0 with back-to-back cash.
- Highways connect each stop to the nearest stop in the next column, with a 35% chance of a second road, so routes branch and cross.
- Reachable stops pulse; click to travel. Your route is drawn in red marker; HERE marks your current stop. A sticky note shows cash and gigs played (of 4). *View binder* works on the map (button or B).
- Target scores grow with gigs played; the headliner's are ×1.25 on top. Finishing the headliner: **Tour complete!** Booed off anywhere: the tour ends. Both return to the main menu.

Stops show only their icon and a name written in marker beside it (venues and the headliner also get the town in red). Hovering a gig or headliner pops up its card with every like and hate spelled out in full ("LIKES · +3 crowd each" and "HATES · -3 crowd each"), so the cards never cover other stops.

The other stops reveal one thing each, and it is always true: a **pickup** shows either a genre (colored label, name written under it) or a decade on its box, and one of its three choices matches; a **record shop** reads "Has Metal 00s" (an album of that genre and decade is in stock) or "Has Compressor" (that gear is in stock, unless you already own it, in which case it shows the album hint instead).

## Pickups and cash
Black screen, a spotlight from above, and the reward on a pool of light.
- **Pickup:** a taped cardboard box marked FRAGILE. **Click it three times** to open it (it shakes, the lid lifts and the tape tears; three dots track progress). Inside are **three choices, and you take one**: usually three albums of different genres (any points, maybe with an edition), but when you have an empty rack bay one of the three is a gear unit 30% of the time, and always after two pickups in a row without gear (a pity counter that resets each run). Each choice has its own *Take this CD* / *Install this* button, or *Leave them*. On the map each pickup box carries a **stamp**: either a genre (a colored label with its short code, the full name written under it) or a decade, and **one of the three choices always matches** it, so you can route toward what the next crowd likes.
- **Cash:** a tip jar full of bills. Click once to collect $4–8.

## Scoring
*Parts of this and the next section describe the old 6-slot set. Where they disagree with "The gig (turns)" above, that section wins.*

**Lay out the whole set, then press PLAY.** You fill all 6 slots of the changer from your hand, in any order. When you press PLAY the set resolves left to right, one song at a time, and every card's effect fires in turn. There is no hit or miss: every card always scores, and a set-wide **flow** builds up as the set plays. The gig is cleared if your total is at or above the target score after song 6, and you are booed off if it isn't. The target grows over the tour (`GIG_TARGETS`: 60, 110, 200, 360, 650, 1150; a boss gig is x1.25 as a headliner, and the "High expectations" boss another x1.3).

**Flow is a running multiplier. A song scores (points + additions) x flow x local multipliers.** Flow starts at x1 each show and persists through the set. Effects raise it after their song plays, so builders go early and your big-point songs go late, building to a finale. Scores can get very large and are shown with K and M suffixes above 100,000.
- **Points:** every album has a number set by its effect (`COND_POINTS`): a plain card 12, an effect card 4 to 8.
- **Every card's rule has three parts: a Condition, an Effect and an Effect target.** The slot grid is a snake, so "above" means the song in the slot over it on slots 4 to 6.
  - **Condition** (when it fires): Always; Played first; Played last; In the first set (slots 1 to 3); In the second set (slots 4 to 6); In an odd slot; In an even slot; In exactly slot 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6; The song before is a genre or decade; The song above is a genre or decade (slots 4 to 6 only); The song after is a genre or decade; For each earlier song of a genre or decade (fires once per matching song, and the effect scales with the count); For each song of a genre or decade left in your hand (the hand is 8 and the set is 6, so at most 2 songs are left, and the effect scales with the count).
  - **Effect** (what happens, with a value): No effect; Add points; Multiply the score; Raise flow; Multiply flow; Add cash (you earn the value in dollars when the song plays; with a per-song condition it scales with the count); Copy the previous song's effect.
  - **Effect target** (who it happens to): This song; The next song; Every later song; Later songs of one genre; Later songs of one decade; or, for the two flow effects, the whole set (flow is applied after the song and carries on).
  - Points and multiplier effects can target this song, the next song or later songs; flow effects always target the set. Effects only reach forward, never back to earlier songs, so the set still resolves left to right.
  - The old fixed effects are presets of this: Set 1 +10 is (In the first set, Add 10 points, This song); Last x3 is (Played last, Multiply x3, This song); After Punk x2 is (The song before is Punk, Multiply x2, This song); Amp is (Always, Raise flow 1.5, the set); Surge is (Always, Multiply flow x2.5, the set); Fans is (For each earlier Punk song, Raise flow 0.5, the set); Anthem is (Always, Add 8 points, Later Punk songs); Copy is (Always, Copy).
  - A card with no effect is plain (Tube Preamp adds to these). Pills on the disc and tooltips are generated from the three parts, for example "SET 1 +10", "AFTER PUNK x2", "FLOW +1.5", "LATER PUNK +8" or "NEXT +10". When a card buffs another song, that song lists a "From <title>" line.
- **Additions** are added to the points and **multipliers** multiply the total, in any order: a liked genre or decade +5, the request +10 (the first song of the requested genre or decade in your set), editions (Holographic +3, Signed +5, Limited +8), gear (see Rack), a hated song x0.5 (nothing on the "Angry crowd" boss), a boss debuff on your most common genre or decade x0.5, and a boss's locked slot scores nothing for songs that don't fit.
- The lines of each song float up one by one with the running total on its slot as it plays, so you can see what fired. Nothing in the game shows what a set will score before you press PLAY: you read the cards, the slot and neighbour readout on the rack, and work it out.

**Pay:** a missed target pays $2 and ends the tour. A cleared target pays $5 ("Target cleared"), $8 at 1.5x the target ("Big applause") and $11 at 2x ("Encore!"). The meter on the rack shows your score against the target with ticks at 1.5x and 2x.

## The show
- Your binder is shuffled at the start of each show. You draw a **hand of 6** into the CD wallet (two rows of 3, `HAND`), and nothing is drawn after that: you play all 6 of them, choosing the order.
- The changer has **6 slots in a 3×2 snake** (`SETLIST`): slots 1 to 3 run left to right along the top row, then slots 4 to 6 run right to left along the bottom, so slot 4 sits under 3 and slot 6 sits under slot 1. They are played in order, one at a time. Only the open slot takes a CD:
  - Click a CD in your hand to load it into the first empty slot. Click a CD in the changer to pick it up (the slot glows gold), then click another slot to swap or move it, a hand pocket to swap it with that CD, or the same CD again to send it back to your hand. Right-click cancels. PLAY unlocks when all 6 slots are full.
  - Slots show their score only once played; the arrows between slots and the link icons between rows light up when the song's effect fired.
  - PLAY: Mixes become their genre and decade, then the set resolves left to right, 4 seconds a song. Each song floats up its lines (effect, points, additions, multipliers) with a running total, then locks showing e.g. "+168". The show ends after slot 8.
- **Card look (jewel case):** every CD in the hand and on the changer is drawn with the shop's jewel case template at 232 px wide: a silver rim, a black case with the brushed spine on the left (always black), and a square cover whose background is the genre color (Rock blue, Pop pink, Hip-Hop purple, Jazz green; `SLEEVE_*` in the data file) with the CD and its artwork on it and no labels on the CD. A genre-colored title strip runs the full width across the very top of the case (no margin, no border, a subtle drop shadow below it, no icon) with the song name in a large font that starts after the points badge; a small white decade sticker sits on the right of the CD. The rest of the information is three round charcoal-rimmed badges sitting on the case's corners and spilling off its edge, like Hearthstone's stat gems, so they read as interface rather than part of the case: the **top left** badge is the base points (always green, white number); the **bottom left** badge is the placement condition (charcoal, white, one short line); the **bottom right** badge is the reward (green for points, purple for flow, gold for cash). Only a type shows on the reward badge: a note for points, a wave for flow, a big white $ for cash (no circle around it), with an "x" in front for a multiplier and a small chevron (next) or double chevron (later) when it buffs other songs; the amount is in the tooltip. **Condition badge key:** a number for a slot ("1" opener, "6" closer, "4"), a range for a set ("1-3", "4-6"), "O" odd, "E" even, an arrow plus a one-letter genre or two-digit decade for a neighbor (←J: the song before it is Jazz, ↑P: the song above it is Pop, →H: the song after it is Hip-Hop, ←= copies the song before it), ∀R for each Rock before it, a card fan plus a letter for each in your hand, and "?" for a Mix CD. Plain cards with no effect show no condition or reward badge. The icons are drawn in code. Editions add a small pill; a playing CD stays in its slot dimmed and marked PLAYING. **One template everywhere:** the same jewel case is used for every CD that is not playing: the hand, the changer, the shop (with a price sticker at the right end of the title strip), the binder, album packs, the sneak peek and the pickup choices. The only art-only discs are the CD spinning in the player during a gig and the record on the title screen, which show just the artwork with no labels.
- **Changer slots:** each slot is a larger jewel case (232 wide) with its number to the left in a small badge that lights green while the card's condition is met by the current arrangement (and stays dim for cards with no condition or no effect). There are no arrows or link symbols between slots; the numbers show the play order (left to right on the top row, right to left on the bottom). Under each case is a small LCD like the stereo's points and flow boxes showing the song's running total as it plays.
- **Drag and drop:** press a case in the hand or the changer and move a few pixels to pick it up. It lifts and grows with a shadow, follows the cursor, and the slot it came from shows an empty ring. Every other slot gets a faint gold ring, and the one under the cursor lights up (a generous snap radius, no pixel hunting). Release on a slot to drop: an occupied slot swaps (the other CD slides to where yours came from), and hand to hand, hand to changer, changer to changer and changer back to hand all work. Release anywhere else, or press Escape or right-click, and the CD slides back. The cursor becomes a hand over a CD and a grabbing hand while dragging, and tooltips hide while dragging. A plain click still works as before (click a hand CD to fill the next free slot, click a changer CD then another to swap). Dragging is off while a set plays or any popup is open.
- **Discard** (2 per show, more with gear such as the tuner): throws away your whole hand (every CD in the wallet, not the ones on the changer) and draws a fresh hand of 6. If the binder runs short, the older discards are reshuffled in, and if it is still short the CDs you just threw away fill the gaps. The older version, which `DIG_PICK` in the data file brings back (set it to true), was: throw away 1 CD from your hand, then **choose 1 of 3** from a panel that covers only the gear rack, so the changer, your hand (the empty slot glows gold), the score panel and the venue clipboard all stay visible and hoverable. While choosing, PLAY, placing and discarding are locked CDs drawn at random from the draw pile. The other two go back into the pile. The discarded CD goes to a discard pile and can't be offered again until the draw pile runs low (fewer than 3 left), when the discard pile is shuffled back in. Discards are free; they are how you dig for the cards your sequence needs. If the pile is empty, discarding is unavailable.
- **Draw pile table:** a readout in the console strip, next to the View binder button, shows how many CDs of each genre and each decade are still in the draw pile (amber digits, genre-colored underline). There is no text summary and no switch. The genre and decade of the song just played glow cyan, since those are the tags that keep the flow. Hovering a CD in your hand or the changer adds "Left in the draw pile: N of its genre, M of its decade" to its tooltip.
- **Reading the slot:** the display on the rack shows the slot you have picked up, are hovering over or (while playing) are resolving: its set, odd or even, first or last, and the genre and decade of the song before it (PREV), above it (ABOVE) and after it (NEXT), so you can check an effect before you commit.
- At the start of each show the crowd shouts a **request** ("Play some 90s!"): +10 points for the first song of that genre or decade in your set.
- The venue's likes, hates and the current request live on the sticky paper at the top of the stereo.
- **Console strip** (bottom, brushed metal like the rack units), left to right: a black **View binder** button, the **draw pile table** (see Discard) and the **Sort** group: four stereo-style push keys in a row (Genre, Decade, Points, Ability), one pressed in at a time with an amber lamp on the pressed key; pressing the pressed key again goes back to the order you drew them in. Ability groups the hand by reward type: points, x points, flow, x flow, cash, copies, no effect, then Mix CDs. There is no sort by band.
- **Action panel** (bottom right, under the gear rack, brushed metal): a large green **PLAY SET** button, a large red **Discard** button with an amber light for each discard left, with the loaded count (or the slot being played) and a one-line hint underneath. The play button no longer lives in the stereo.

## Boss gigs
**The third gig of every lane is a boss** (boss and headliner mean the same thing): gigs 3 and 6. Beating gig 6 completes the run. Bosses use the headliner's venue and ×1.25 targets, and carry one **challenge**; the two lanes in a fork get different ones. The fork sign names each lane's challenge, a boss screen spells it out, and the crowd panel keeps a red reminder. The challenges (`BOSS_KINDS`):
- **Genre debuffed / Decade debuffed:** songs of your most common genre (or decade) score half all night.
- **Crowd request:** one of slots 2-5 only takes a chosen genre or decade; anything else scores nothing. A red tag marks the slot.
- **Angry crowd:** an extra hated genre (one of your common ones), and hated songs score nothing.
- **High expectations:** the target is 30% higher.
- **Broken equipment:** one piece of rack gear is out for the show (restored afterward).
- **Rough crowd:** each song played has a 20% chance to be destroyed for good (removed from the binder when you leave the show).
- **No time to dig:** no discards.


## Starting kits
The game opens on a title screen (Play, Settings), on a plain black background, showing the same square CD player used for the CD now playing in a gig, with a random album's cover spinning in it (art only, no labels). The kit screen is also on plain black. Then a **kit screen** with four cards. Each kit is one piece of starting gear for the rack, and every kit starts with $0. There is no Basement any more. Picking a kit opens the **deck screen** (see Deck), and picking a deck starts the run at the first fork. Winning or losing returns to the title.

**Music.** One song from `background_music/` (`BG_DIR`) plays on a loop in the background on every screen, from the title screen through the road, the shop and the planning part of a gig, at a low level (`BG_DB`). It fades out and pauses whenever another song needs to be heard: while you hover an album in the shop or in an album pack (that album plays from a random point and fades out when you move away; the background then resumes where it paused) and while a gig's set plays (from the 3-2-1 countdown until the last song). When the set is over, the background music stays silent through the end-of-show screens and comes back when you leave the gig for the road or the shop. If the folder holds several songs they play in turn. **Countdown:** pressing PLAY SET starts a quick 3-2-1 (`COUNT_STEP` seconds each, with a rising tick) over the changer before the first song starts.
- **Deck:** you pick it on a **deck screen** after the kit screen. The decks are data in `data/decks.json`, edited in the **deck workshop** (`deck_workshop.tscn`). Each deck has a name, a one-line description, an enabled flag, a number of **Mixes** (blank CD-Rs), and a number of copies of each card in the catalog (`data/cards.json`, by card id). The deck screen shows every enabled deck (up to `DECKS_SHOWN`, 12) with the cover of its most-copied card, its CD and Mix counts, and its description. Picking one starts the run with exactly those cards and Mixes. Cards that no longer exist are skipped. With no enabled deck the game offers one deck of every card. This replaces the old genre and decade decks.
- **Kits** (`KITS` in the data file):
  - **Chain Gang:** Echo Unit.
  - **Showstopper:** Make Some Noise.
  - **Genre Streak:** Gain Stage, dialed to Pop.
  - **Crowd Pleaser:** Tube Preamp.
- **Mixes** (blank CD-Rs, 3 points, no effect): at PLAY a Mix picks a random album from the whole catalog and becomes that album's genre and decade for the neighbours, crowd and gear. It resets after each show.
- Tuning knobs: `KITS`, `DECKS_SHOWN`, and the decks themselves (`data/decks.json`). The kits differ only by gear for now.

Better albums, editions and more gear come from pickups and the shop.

## The catalog and effects
**Every CD is a persistent card with a fixed identity**, stored in `data/cards.json` (63 cards today, 7 genres x 3 bands x 3 albums). Each card has: `id`, `title`, `band`, `cover` (artwork path), `song` (music path), `genre`, `decade`, `points`, `condition` and `cond_param` (the genre or decade a condition looks at, such as "Punk"), `effect` and `value`, `target` and `target_param` (the genre or decade for later-song targets), and `rarity` (common, uncommon, rare). The game loads this file at startup (`_load_albums`); if it is missing it falls back to the old generated catalog (`_legacy_albums`). Nothing about a card is rolled at runtime except its **edition** (8% of found cards).

**Where cards come from.** Starting decks list their cards exactly (see Deck). The shop, pickups and packs roll a rarity with `RARITY_WEIGHTS` (20% common, 55% uncommon, 25% rare) and then pick a card of that rarity, the first one matching the road's genre or decade when there is a hint. Duplicates of cards you own can show up. The first-draft catalog (`tools/make_cards.gd`) gave 38 commons (14 plain at 5 points, simple effects at 3), 17 uncommons (plain 9, effects 5; Above, Fans, Anthem, Amp) and 8 rares (plain 14, effects 7; Surge, Copy, Last, Anthem), with Fans and Anthem aimed at the card's own genre. It is a starting point to hand-tune in the workshop.

**Card workshop** (`card_workshop.tscn`, run it with F6 in the editor): the tool for editing the catalog.
- **Layout:** a top bar (title, a checks badge, status, Reload, Save), then three columns, then a dock along the bottom.
  - Left, the **library**: artwork thumbnails, search, filters for genre, decade, condition, effect and rarity (with Clear filters), and New card, Duplicate and Delete.
  - Centre, the **card preview**, large and centred, drawn with the game's own disc code (the tape label across the top reads the card's title here, instead of the game's "Decade-Genre") (big, and again at changer and hand size) with its effect text, and below it a **rule summary**: the generated description and a power bar (green, blue or pink for the suggested common, uncommon or rare).
  - Right, the **inspector**, in sections: Identity (id, title, band, genre, decade, rarity), Scoring (points), Rule (Condition, Condition target, Effect, Effect value, Effect target, Target filter) and Assets (artwork and music with Browse, Play and Stop). The dependent dropdowns follow the choices: the condition target appears only for conditions that look at a genre or decade, the effect target list only offers valid targets for the chosen effect, and the target filter appears for later-genre and later-decade targets.
  - Bottom dock, the **catalog summary**: a genre-by-decade coverage table drawn as a heat map (red for empty, blue for 1 to 2, green for 3 or more, with row, column and grand totals; click a cell to filter the library to it), three rarity cards (count and average power), and the **checks** list (missing artwork or music, duplicates, missing targets, zero values, invalid targets; click a warning to open that card).
- **Drag and drop:** drop one image on the window to set the selected card's artwork, or several images to create a new card for each (named from "Band - Title.jpg"); drop an mp3 to attach it to the selected card or to the card whose name it matches. Files are copied into `art/covers` and `music/<genre>`.
- Ctrl+S saves `cards.json` (it is one file, written in tab-indented JSON, so it diffs well in git). The workshop only edits the project folder when run from the editor.

**Genre and decade** are one of each per card and drive the crowd, gear, effects and bosses: a venue like adds +5 points for a matching genre or decade, a hated one halves the song, the request is met by a matching song, Graphic EQ adds the genre's fader, Gain Stage and Exciter add for their dial's genre or decade, and boss genre and decade debuffs apply to matching songs. Genre has no colour anywhere. A Mix copies a random card's cover, genre and decade when it is played.

**Rules:** a card can be played any number of times in a set, and the same card can be owned more than once.

**Editions** (8% of albums): Holographic +3 points, Signed +5, Limited Edition +8, Mint Condition +$1 per play.

## Rack
The rack has 3 bays. A Full unit fills a bay and a Half unit takes half of one, so a bay holds one Full unit or two Half units (6 half-slots, `RACK_SLOTS`). The full roster, with every unit's numbers, is in `GEAR.md` and `D.GEAR`. The kit gives one unit; more comes from pickups and the shop.
- **Chain order:** the signal runs left to right through a bay, then down to the next bay. Splitter duplicates the next unit in the chain and Wah Pedal counts the units before it. A unit duplicated by Splitter applies its bonus a second time (+3 becomes +6, x2 becomes x3, $5 becomes $10, active units get a second use, Octave and Sampler use a second random pair).
- **Rearranging:** click a unit (or press and drag it) and click another slot to move it, or swap it with the unit there, until PLAY is pressed. A Half unit can swap with a Full unit only if its bay has a free half-slot. Right-click or Escape cancels.
- **Installing:** a new unit goes into a slot you pick. A Half unit replaces whatever is in that slot and a Full unit replaces the unit in a bay that holds at most one. In the shop, **SELL** on a unit pays half its price (a Piggy Bank also pays its pot).
- **Active gear** (CD Burner, Deep Library, Sneak Peek) has a USE button that works while you lay out the set, once per gig (twice next to a Splitter). Deep Library redraws your whole hand, Sneak Peek shows the next 5 songs in the binder, and the CD Burner turns a Mix in your hand into a copy of an album in your hand.
- **Scaling gear:** Vintage Amp (60s songs) and Feedback (Rock songs) store +0.1 flow for each matching song you play and add it to the starting flow of every set for the rest of the run. Piggy Bank gains $1 each gig and pays out when sold or when you beat a boss.
- **Money gear:** Merch Table ($5), Tip Jar ($2 per unused discard) and Piggy Bank pay when the show ends ("+$ gear" on the end screen); Radio, Sampler and T Shirt Cannon pay as songs play.
- **Crowd gear:** Noise Gate ignores one randomly chosen hated tag each gig (it does nothing when the boss has the hates ability); Request Line makes requests pay +20.
- **Boss "broken equipment"** switches one unit off for the show (it shows BROKEN and is skipped by the chain).
- **Tube Preamp** counts the different genres across your whole hand of 6 (the songs in the changer and the ones still in your hand).
- Graphic EQ now comes as Genre EQ and Decade EQ. A work order tweak raises one fader by a step on either.

## Gear data and the gear workshop
Every gear unit lives in `data/gear.json`: id, name, text, size (full or half), price, panel look, whether it has a button, and its numbers (`params`). The game reads that file on start (`_load_gear`) and looks numbers up with `_gp` (a number) and `_gs` (a genre or decade name). Text can hold placeholders such as `+{points} points per song`, which fill in from the unit's numbers. Twenty units are built from a **rule** (`"rule"` in the file) and the game runs them with one shared evaluator: Overdrive, Make Some Noise, Reverb, Stack Amp, Radio, Turntables, Fog Machine, Phaser, Looper, Delay Pedal, Wah Pedal, Merch Table, Tip Jar and Tuner, plus six drafts (disabled): Blues Drive, Chorus, Funky Groove, Showtune, Distortion and Shred. A rule has: **when** (each song, the opener, the closing song, start of the gig, end of the gig), **fires for** (any song, or songs of one genre or decade: the song that receives the effect), **only if** (nothing; the song before shares its decade; the last two songs share a genre; it shares a genre or decade with the opener; **adjacent**, a neighbor on either side is a chosen genre; **streak**, it is every Nth song in a row of its own genre, so with N = 3 it fires on the 3rd and 6th and starts counting again after a break; **match back**, it shares its genre with the song N back), an **effect** (add points, multiply the score, raise flow, add cash, add discards), an **amount**, **multiplied by** (nothing, each earlier song of the same genre, each other song of that genre in the set, each unit to its left, each unused discard), a **count limit**, and a short **label** shown on the song. Next to a Splitter the amount applies a second time (a multiplier of 2 becomes 3). The inspector shows each rule as one sentence (for example "Fires for Rock songs if adjacent to a Jazz song: +5 points."). Adjacent counts the Saxophone and Octave genre swaps. The other units still have their behavior written in code, so they only have numbers, text, price, size and look to edit.

Run `gear_workshop.tscn` (F6) to edit the file. It is laid out like the card workshop:
- **Library** (left): every unit with search and filters for size and kind (active or passive).
  - **New unit** adds a rule unit (each song, +3 points) named "New unit", which is ready to edit. **Duplicate** copies the selected rule unit with a new id. Units with behavior written in code (Octave, the EQs and so on) cannot be duplicated, because their behavior is tied to their id. **Delete** asks first, and refuses a unit that a starting kit uses. New and duplicated units start disabled. The **Enabled** checkbox decides whether a unit can appear in shops, pickups and hints; a disabled unit stays in the file as a draft (marked "(draft)" in the list), and a "State" filter shows only enabled or only disabled units. Units with no flag count as enabled. A disabled unit that a starting kit uses is flagged in the checks.
- **Preview** (center): the unit drawn by the game's own rack code, with its price and sell value, and the text with the numbers filled in.
- **Inspector** (right): name, text, bay size, price and panel look, plus either the unit's numbers (a genre or decade picker for units tied to one) or, for the rule units, the rule dropdowns described above. Combinations that cannot work (for example a multiplier at the end of a gig) show up in Checks. The kind and a note on what the code does are shown but not editable. Ctrl+S saves.
- **Balance test** (bottom left): each unit alone, on 60 random hands from the card catalog, scored by the real scorer. The table shows points lift, cash per gig (Merch Table, Tip Jar and Piggy Bank count their end-of-gig money), a value that counts $1 as a number of points you choose (default 3), and value per dollar of price. Wah Pedal is tested with three units to its left, Splitter with an Overdrive to its right, and the counter units with 10 stored. Units whose effect isn't a scoring bonus (buttons, rule changes, Tuner, Request Line, Noise Gate) show as "utility".
- **Checks** (bottom right): missing or duplicate names, text placeholders with no matching number, numbers not shown in the text, text too long for a Half unit, a Full unit with no panel look, genre units whose genre has no cards in the catalog, and units whose value per dollar is more than twice or under half the median. Click one to jump to the unit.


## Pay and the shop
After each show: booed $2, polite clapping $5, Big applause $8, Encore $11, plus Mint Condition money.

**The B-Side** (a record shop stop in each reward stretch), restocked at random each visit. The screen has a sign across the top (the shop name; while you are installing gear it reads "Choose a rack bay for the ...") and three aisles side by side, left to right: **Records**, **Gear** and **Work orders**. The right column is fixed and shared with the gig screen: a **cash** readout at the top, the **rig** (6 half racks) under it, and a panel at the bottom with a large green *Exit shop* button (back to the road) and a smaller *View binder* button. In a gig the same bottom panel holds *Play set* and *Discard*, and the cash readout sits above the rig there too. Buying albums, album packs and gear boxes is **press and hold** (0.6 seconds, with a gold fill rising over the item and a "Hold to buy" label on hover); releasing early cancels. For gear, clicking the unit still picks it up, and you press and hold on the rack bay to pay and install it.
- **Records** (left aisle, jewel cases on crate shelves with price stickers; hover one to hear it): 2 albums ($3–5), one of them matching the map's hint if it was an album hint, plus an album pack (Sampler: pick 1 of 3 for $4, or Box Set: pick 2 of 4 for $7).
- **Gear** (middle aisle, a pegboard with price tags): 2 units you don't own (one is the hinted unit if the map's hint was gear and you don't own it yet) plus a gear box (Grab Box: pick 1 of 3 for $6, or Studio Bundle: pick 2 of 3 for $11).
- **Work orders** (right aisle, a workbench with up to two order tickets stacked, and a ledger showing binder size, shows played and rig units): bench tweak ($3), upgrade a unit ($6), or give an album an edition.
- **Opening a pack** (Sampler, Box Set, Grab Box, Studio Bundle): clicking a pack plays a short show. The screen fades to black and everything disappears, including the cash readout and the rig, which sit behind a small arrow tab on the right edge (click it to open the cash and the rig as a full-height panel, for example to sell a piece of gear, and click the arrow again to close it). The pack, its contents, the title and the Skip button are centered on the screen, and slide left to stay clear of the panel while it is open. The pack appears in the center and shakes harder and harder while swirls of light and glowing particles orbit it and spiral inward (`PACK_BUILD`, about 2.3 seconds, with rising ticks). It then bursts: a flash of light, rays, expanding shock rings, a thud and a spray of particles with a small screen shake. The contents then appear one at a time (`PACK_GAP` apart): each album case or piece of gear scales up from nothing with a spring and a burst of particles. The items are scaled as a whole picture, so their text stays steady. **Rarity** (`PACK_RARITY`) is shown by the light behind an item, not by the item itself: common items get a soft white glow; uncommon items get a cyan glow that pulses, a ring and a higher tick; rare items get a large gold glow and slowly turning rays that pulse harder, a ring, a burst of gold sparks, a thud and a little screen shake. Albums use their catalog rarity; gear has no rarity field, so it is taken from its price ($4–5 common, $6 uncommon, $7 and up rare). When the last item has appeared, the screen reads "Hold to take" and you **press and hold** an item (`HOLD_TIME`, 0.6 seconds, with a gold fill rising over it) to take it; releasing early cancels. A taken item is dimmed and marked TAKEN, and the pack closes when you have taken your picks. There is no Add button, only **Skip**, which closes the pack and keeps anything you already took. Clicking before the items have all appeared skips straight to this state. Hovering an album plays it, as in the shop.
- *View binder* works in the shop too.

## Balance
The scoring is now a laid-out sequence (see Scoring), so the old per-venue table is gone.


Measured over 150 hands of 8 at random venues (no gear), with starting decks built from the persistent catalog's commons (all 13 decks, picked at random): picking and ordering 6 cards at random scores a median of about 49 (p10 31, p90 73); the 6 highest-point cards in hand order about 48; and a hill-climbing planner that searches for the best 6 and order scores about 82 (p10 57, p90 154). Targets (60, 110, 200, 360, 650, 1150, about 1.8x per gig) are a first pass: the first gig is clearable with the starting deck, and later gigs assume you have found better CDs and gear. The shop and pickups haven't been simulated. Tuning knobs: `GIG_TARGETS`, `HEADLINER_MULT`, `BOSS_TARGET`, `RARITY_WEIGHTS`, the catalog itself (`data/cards.json`), `AMP_ADD`, `SURGE_MULT`, `FANS_FLOW`, `ECHO_FLOW`, `SET_BONUS`, `SIDE_BONUS`, `OPENER_MULT`, `CLOSER_MULT`, `AFTER_MULT`, `ABOVE_MULT`, `ANTHEM_ADD`, `HATE_MULT`, `LIKE`, `REQUEST`, `EQ_UNIT`, `HAND`, `SETLIST`, `TIER_GOOD`, `TIER_GREAT`, `PAY_*`, `CDR_POINTS`, `MUSIC_SECONDS`.

## Music

Pressing PLAY starts the album's track from a random point (at least a second before the end) and lets it run 4 seconds, fading over the last 0.8. The song only finishes, and the next one can be played, once both the score readout and the 5 seconds are done. Tracks live in `music/<Genre>/<Band>/` and are matched to albums by the file name starting with "Band - Album". A Mix plays the track of the random album it picked. Length is `MUSIC_SECONDS` in the data file.

## Ring demo

`ring_demo.tscn` is a separate prototype scene; the main round is untouched. Open it in the editor and run the scene (F6). It shows a 7×6 genre × decade LED map beside a 5-slot ring changer. Click a CD in the hand to play it into the next slot. The map numbers the set's path, glows the last CD's row and column (they keep the flow) and the opener's row and column, and marks cells that would close the ring. Closing the ring (the last CD shares a genre or decade with the opener) adds +1 flow and lifts the cap to ×5. Label maker mode cycles a CD's opener/closer tags. Deal 3×3 uses a starter-style patch of the map; Deal wide spreads the hand over the whole map.

## Card tags

Each kind of information has one visual channel, and nothing else uses it.

- **Cover and condition:** the disc is the album's cover art. A strip of masking tape across the top reads "Decade-Genre" (for example "80s-Indie") in black marker; if a venue likes or hates that genre or decade, or a gear unit boosts it, that half of the label turns green, red or gold. A dark pill near the bottom of the disc names its effect (SET 2 +10, LAST x3, AFTER PUNK x2, NEXT +12, +4 PER ROCK, COPY PREV and so on). Points sit in the green badge at the top left. There are no chips under the disc and genre has no colour. A Mix shows a blank CD-R until it is played, then the cover it became with a MIX pill.
- **No situation states:** cards in your hand are never highlighted or debuffed; the card shows its effect and you decide.

The old FLOW +1 tag and the old ability icons are gone.

## Art
Drawn in code in a physical style. Optional textures in `art/textures/`: `metal_brushed`, `metal_dark`, `rubber`, `leather`, `fabric`, `wood`, `crate_wood`, `pegboard`, `cardboard`.

Cover loading: source photos can be thousands of pixels wide, so the first time a cover is needed it is shrunk to 768 px and saved as a JPG in `art/thumbs/` (the folder has a `.gdignore`, so Godot does not import it). Later loads read the small file, about 7 ms each instead of up to 1.6 s. All album covers are warmed one per frame at startup. Delete `art/thumbs/` to regenerate.

## Deck workshop

`deck_workshop.tscn` edits `data/decks.json`, like the gear workshop edits gear. The left list holds the decks (state filter All / Enabled / Disabled, New deck, Duplicate, Delete; new and duplicated decks start disabled, shown as "(draft)"). The right side sets the name, description, Enabled, and Mixes, then shows every card in the catalog with a Copies column to edit. A search box, a genre filter and "Only cards in this deck" narrow the card list, and a summary shows CD count, average base points, and the genre and decade mix. Checks flag empty names, duplicate names, decks with no cards or fewer than a hand of 6, cards that no longer exist (shown in red as "(missing)"; set to 0 to drop them), enabled decks with no description, and too many enabled decks. Save with Ctrl+S.
